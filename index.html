Hi, thanks for stopping by.

I'm a Ph.D. student at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, advised by Prof. Pingfeng Wang. I also received my M.S. in Electrical & Computer Engineering from UIUC in 2024, and before that worked on industrial anomaly detection at the Hong Kong Industrial Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Centre.

My research asks how a battery management system can stay resource-efficient, adaptive, and provably safe at the same time. Real BMS hardware runs on small microcontrollers and thin communication links, battery dynamics drift as cells age, and a safety-critical system can't explore its way into lithium plating or thermal runaway to learn a better policy. I build methods that handle all three together, from quantized state-of-health estimation on the edge to online learning with safety certificates, and up to guarantees that hold when many cells are coupled into a pack.

If you work on batteries, prognostics, or safe learning for cyber-physical systems, I'd be glad to hear from you.

Research Interests

  • 1
    Embedded & quantized state estimation: state-of-health tracking with Kalman filters at 8, 16 and 32-bit precision for microcontroller BMS.
  • 2
    Edge-to-cloud battery telemetry: risk-aware, tiered packet quantization that spends bandwidth where it carries prognostic value.
  • 3
    Safe online learning: bandit-based charging and thermal strategy selection with adaptive safety certificates that tighten as cells age.
  • 4
    Federated battery health modeling: training Li-ion health models across fleets without sharing raw data, robust to drift and intermittent connectivity.
  • 5
    Composability of safety in coupled systems: when per-cell guarantees carry over to a pack, and when a single failure can spread to its neighbors.

Publications Google Scholar

  1. RAMS

    Distributed Battery Prognostics via Quantized Edge Encoding and Cloud-Based Feature Decoding

    Lakshmi Manoj and Pingfeng Wang

    Annual Reliability and Maintainability Symposium (RAMS), 2026

    An on-board filter tracks state of health and feeds a health-risk score built from estimation confidence, degradation rate, filter surprise and fault probability. The score selects a telemetry tier, so the edge sends coarse quantized packets while the cell is healthy and richer ones as risk rises. A cloud-side multi-task model decodes features from those packets for prognostics.
  2. RAMS

    On-Board SOH Estimation Using Quantized Adaptive Dual Extended Kalman Filter in Resource-Constrained Battery Management Systems

    Lakshmi Manoj and Pingfeng Wang

    Annual Reliability and Maintainability Symposium (RAMS), 2025

    A dual extended Kalman filter for battery state of health, reduced for microcontroller execution. A monotonic-fade prior keeps estimates consistent with electrochemical capacity loss, and the filter state is quantized to 8, 16 or 32 bits to measure the memory-accuracy trade-off on embedded hardware.

Ongoing Research

  1. Safety

    Adaptive Safety Certificates for Safe-Optimal Battery Charging Protocol Selection

    Lakshmi Manoj and Pingfeng Wang

    Fast charging trades convenience against lithium plating, thermal runaway and accelerated degradation, and the best protocol shifts as a cell ages. This work pairs an Exp3 bandit learner with an ISS-grounded safety certificate that permanently bans protocols whose risk exceeds an adaptive threshold. Across 72 candidate protocols, the certificate pruned the pool to 66 and held the violation rate at zero for the final 1,200 of 1,500 batches.
  2. Coupling

    When Do Per-Cell Safety Guarantees Compose? Failure Propagation in Coupled Systems

    Lakshmi Manoj and Pingfeng Wang

    For passively coupled components such as cells linked by heat flow, small disturbances cannot cascade regardless of coupling strength. The real risk is a full component failure: a fold-bifurcation threshold k* predicts whether it drags a healthy neighbor down, matching detailed simulation to within half a percent. The same threshold predicts failure spread between steps of an AI agent pipeline.
  3. Federated

    Federated Learning for Distributed Battery Health Modeling

    Lakshmi Manoj and Pingfeng Wang

    A federated pipeline that trains Li-ion health models across clients without sharing raw cycling data. Training logs local–global drift and long-horizon stability to trigger retraining, and compressed, sanity-checked updates keep it robust under intermittent connectivity and heterogeneous client data.
